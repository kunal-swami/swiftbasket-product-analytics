# Root-Cause Summary

## Executive conclusion

Seven-day delivered first-order conversion fell from 9.40% to 8.67% after 15 April: a 0.73-point absolute and 7.8% relative decline. The decline is not explained by one factor.

1. **Composition effect:** Meta Ads increased from 18.3% to 30.1% of acquisition and had the lowest post-change conversion. Holding pre-change channel conversion constant, the mix shift explains approximately 0.33 percentage points.
2. **Within-segment effect:** Conversion also declined inside most channels. This explains approximately 0.40 additional points.
3. **Funnel localization:** checkout-to-payment attempts fell by 4.39 points for carts below ₹199 and 5.97 points for ₹199–₹398, compared with only 0.17 points for ₹399+ carts.
4. **Diagnosis:** evidence is consistent with late disclosure of the revised handling fee creating price friction below the fee-waiver threshold. Channel mix amplified, but did not create, the full decline.

## Observation → segmentation → diagnosis → impact

| Stage | Finding |
|---|---|
| Observation | Seven-day conversion declined 0.73 points after 15 April |
| Segmentation | Decline concentrated below ₹399 and was visible within acquisition channels |
| Diagnosis | Late fee disclosure increased checkout abandonment; lower-intent channel mix amplified the aggregate decline |
| Impact | At post-change acquisition volume, restoring 0.73 points would produce roughly 297 additional converted users per 40.6K acquired users |

## What would strengthen causality

The synthetic generator contains the planted scenario, but a real analyst would not treat pre/post association as definitive causality. Requested evidence would include a staggered city rollout, fee-display logs, checkout usability feedback, support-contact reasons, app-release history and a randomized test of one change at a time.
